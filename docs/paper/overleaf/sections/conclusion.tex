\section{Conclusion}

We presented \system, a cross-layer reference monitor for the point at which a
VLA's continuous output becomes an executable command.  Its central security
object is the exact source \actionblock.  A checker-relative assessment is
bound to that block, its identity is preserved through a one-use execution
transaction, and a triggered physical boundary requires qualified containment
without source-action replacement.  The composition is captured by the
$\mathsf{ProofAligned}$ property, with Lean machine-checking its finite
authorization, ordered-action, receipt, effect, and phase-transition relations.

The complete attack-evaluation protocol measures a 39/86 (45.35\%) observed
risk-transition ASR under SABER.  In the paired four-arm mechanism study,
L1-only records 13/18
attacked task success, both L2-on arms record 0/18 observed joint-limit
violation episodes, and Dual combines the 13/18 and 0/18 sample outcomes.
These outcomes expose the intended separation between task monitoring and
covered physical containment in the frozen simulator setting; they do not
establish population-level effects.

Broader robot-safety claims require trusted perception, typed controller
binding, more attacks and policies, hardware feedback, real-robot validation,
and hard-real-time analysis.  \system instead closes a narrower, auditable
chain: which action was assessed, which authorization permitted it, which
command was applied, and which evidence allowed the execution phase to close.
