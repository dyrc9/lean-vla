\section{Problem Definition and Threat Model}
\label{sec:model}

This section formalizes the lifecycle implied by C1--C3 as a scoped two-stage
property.  \lone binds a checker-relative trusted-task decision to the exact
source block.  \ltwo binds the admitted block to ordered dispatch and evidence
and requires registered guard qualification at a covered boundary.  Universal
robot safety remains outside this property.

\subsection{System Model and Protected Object}

Let $T$ denote trusted task intent, represented by the authoritative task
description.  Intent here names this protected input, not the policy's latent
state.  At policy call $t$, the monitor
reads a trusted observation $O^T_t$ and assigns a unique proposal identity
$\rho_t$ tied to the current episode, policy call, and observation epoch.  The
policy may receive a different, adversary-controlled view
$(P_t^{pol},O_t^{pol},H_t^{pol})$.  Before invoking the policy, the trusted branch
selects and freezes the currently legal semantic subtask $Z_t$; the policy then
returns one source \actionblock:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_t &= \mathsf{Select}(T,O_t^T),\\
A_t &= \pi(P_t^{pol},O_t^{pol},H_t^{pol}),\\
S_t &= \mathsf{Assess}(Z_t,O_t^T,A_t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:proposal-generation}
\end{equation}
Here $S_t$ is a checker-relative assessment, not a claim about the policy's
latent intent.  The selector derives $Z_t$ from a task graph
$\mathcal G_T=(V_T,E_T,\ell_T,\Phi_T)$, where $V_T$ contains task phases,
$E_T$ contains legal phase transitions, $\ell_T$ labels each phase with a
subtask, and $\Phi_T(O_t^T)$ is the state-enabled frontier.  An unresolved
frontier yields an unknown assessment and no permission to dispatch.

The protected object is the ordered source block $A_t$ from
Equation~\ref{eq:action-block-background}.  The monitor associates it with the
assessment $S_t$, an execution contract $C_t$, a one-use permission
$\mathit{Auth}_t$, ordered receipts $R_t$, and post-dispatch effect evidence
$E_t$.  When guard screening is required at step $i$, $g_{t,i}$ records the
selected guard/controller configuration.  A protected step is consequently
identified by
\begin{equation}
(\rho_t,A_t,i,g_{t,i}).
\label{eq:protected-step}
\end{equation}
The source row $a_{t,i}$ is unchanged at the protected controller boundary;
$g_{t,i}$ may change the controller-to-motion mapping.  Equality of source
actions therefore does not imply equality of physical trajectories.

\subsection{Adversary, Goal, and Scope}

At the \intentaction stage, the adversary controls policy-facing language,
visual input, and history and may adapt these inputs after observing earlier
outputs.  The empirical study exercises the SABER instruction channel; the
other context channels remain threat-model scope without end-to-end evaluation.
The policy remains an untrusted proposer even when its checkpoint is fixed.

At the \actionexecution stage, the adversary controls software outside the
trusted computing base (TCB).  After the policy returns, it may substitute a
source block, replay an earlier permission, reorder or omit steps, reuse
evidence across proposals, suppress evidence, or request an unsupported phase
advance.  These are the transaction faults targeted by \ltwoa.  \ltwob covers
state-dependent realization at the registered joint boundary; this risk need
not require a separate attacker action.

A lifecycle security violation occurs if the protected boundary issues
permission for an
ineligible proposal, accepts a step that is not the next authorized source row,
closes a transaction with missing or mismatched evidence, advances the trusted
task phase without observed completion, or dispatches a triggered step without
the registered guard qualification.  Evidence suppression and denial of service
are observable failures but are not prevented; the monitor responds by leaving
the transaction incomplete or refusing dispatch.

The model does not cover compromise of the TCB, deceptive trusted sensors or
actuator feedback, an unmediated physical actuator path, hardware faults,
side-channel leakage, or arbitrary physical hazards outside the registered
checker and guard predicates.  It also does not assume that the adversary can
break the protected state used to distinguish proposals and consume a
permission once.

\subsection{Trusted Computing Base and Evidence Endpoints}

The TCB contains the authoritative task source, trusted observation tap,
task-graph selector, local checker, transaction monitor, guard installation and
snapshot/restore path, sole actuator-facing boundary, effect observer, and
protected monitor state.  The policy, its input view, and its source block are
untrusted.  The guarantee assumes correct TCB execution, complete mediation of
all actuator commands, truthful trusted observations and feedback, and protected
monitor state.

Three endpoints give the evidence chain its meaning.  The \emph{authority
endpoint} establishes $(T,O_t^T,Z_t)$ before the adversary-controlled policy view
is used.  The \emph{dispatch endpoint} records which source row and execution
configuration the controller accepted.  The \emph{effect endpoint} records the
post-dispatch observation associated with that accepted step.  Claims about task
authority, command acceptance, and effects derive only from their respective
endpoints.

\subsection{Lifecycle Security Objective}

The following requirements map directly to the two stages.  Eligible assessment
is the \lone/C1 obligation.  Authorization and ordered transaction closure are the \ltwoa/C2
obligation, and triggered guard qualification is the \ltwob/C3 obligation.
They are stated over one proposal and composed over the complete runtime trace.

\paragraph{L1 (C1). Eligible assessment}
An assessment is eligible only when it comes from the trusted task branch,
refers to the current proposal and observation epoch, is known, and contains no
covered hard-risk finding:
\begin{align}
\mathsf{Eligible}_t \triangleq{}&
\mathsf{Trusted}(T,O_t^T,Z_t) \land
\mathsf{Current}(\rho_t,O_t^T) \nonumber\\
&\land\mathsf{Binds}(S_t,Z_t,O_t^T,A_t)
\land\mathsf{Known}(S_t) \nonumber\\
&\land\neg\mathsf{HardRisk}(S_t).
\label{eq:eligible}
\end{align}
Task-progress uncertainty is recorded as advisory evidence: it does not
claim completion, and the task phase remains unchanged unless later effect
evidence independently establishes a legal transition.

\paragraph{L2a (C2). One-use ordered execution and closure}
The eligible assessment becomes an input to the execution transaction.
Issuance requires that $\mathit{Auth}_t$ names the same proposal, task anchor,
assessment, contract, and source block, and has not previously been used.
For receipt $r_{t,i}$, define
\begin{align}
\mathsf{ReceiptOK}_{t,i}(r_{t,i}) \triangleq{}&
r_{t,i}.\mathit{proposal}=\rho_t \nonumber\\
&\land r_{t,i}.\mathit{authorization}=\mathit{Auth}_t \nonumber\\
&\land r_{t,i}.\mathit{index}=i \nonumber\\
&\land r_{t,i}.\mathit{source}=a_{t,i}.
\label{eq:receipt-ok}
\end{align}
A live receipt sequence must be a contiguous prefix of the authorized block and
must follow a single opening that consumes the permission before the first
dispatch.  A transaction closes only after every required step has a matching
receipt and the bound effect window is known, complete, and free of forbidden or
unrecognized outcomes:
\begin{align}
\mathsf{CloseOK}_t \triangleq{}&
\mathsf{OpenedOnce}(\mathit{Auth}_t) \nonumber\\
&\land R_t=[r_{t,i}]_{i=0}^{H-1} \nonumber\\
&\land\forall i<H:\mathsf{ReceiptOK}_{t,i}(r_{t,i}) \nonumber\\
&\land\mathsf{EffectsMatch}(E_t,R_t,C_t).
\label{eq:close-ok}
\end{align}
Missing steps, replay, reordering, mismatched evidence, and unknown effects
therefore leave the proposal unclosed and never constitute aligned execution.

\paragraph{L2b (C3). Triggered guard qualification}
Let $m_j$ be the current margin for joint side $j$, and let
$\widehat m_j(g)$ and $\widehat F(g)$ be the registered one-step shadow
measurements for execution configuration $g$.  The evaluated configuration
triggers screening when any of the fourteen joint-side margins is at most
$0.30$ rad.  A configuration qualifies only when it was evaluated from the same
restored pre-step state, receives the same source row, passes the registered
controller check, preserves at least $0.15$ rad predicted margin on every joint
side, and keeps the force proxy at or below 10,000:
\begin{align}
\mathsf{Trig}_{t,i} \triangleq{}&
\min_{1\leq j\leq14}m_j\leq0.30,
\label{eq:guard-trigger}\\
\mathsf{GuardOK}_{t,i}(g) \triangleq{}&
\mathsf{SameSnapshot}(g) \nonumber\\
&\land\mathsf{SameSource}(g,a_{t,i}) \nonumber\\
&\land\mathsf{ControllerOK}(g) \nonumber\\
&\land\min_{1\leq j\leq14}\widehat m_j(g)\geq0.15 \nonumber\\
&\land\widehat F(g)\leq10{,}000.
\label{eq:guard-qualified}
\end{align}
For a triggered step, one qualifying configuration must be recorded after state
restoration and before the dispatch receipt.  If no evaluated configuration
qualifies, no actuator dispatch occurs.

\paragraph{Phase advance and composition}
For task phases $q$ and $q'$, advancement requires both a legal task-graph edge
and observed completion in a closed transaction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{AdvanceOK}_t(q,q')\triangleq{}&
(q,q')\in E_T\land\mathsf{CloseOK}_t\\
&\land\mathsf{CompletionObserved}(q,q',E_t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:advance-ok}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathsf I_\tau$, $\mathsf D_\tau$, $\mathsf C_\tau$, and
$\mathsf A_\tau$ denote issuance, dispatch, close, and phase-advance events in
trace $\tau$.  The top-level target is
\begin{align}
\mathsf{ProofAligned}(\tau)\triangleq{}&
(\forall t\in\mathsf I_\tau:\mathsf{IssueOK}_t) \nonumber\\
&\land(\forall(t,i,r)\in\mathsf D_\tau:
\mathsf{ReceiptOK}_{t,i}(r) \nonumber\\
&\hspace{24mm}\land\mathsf{ScreenOK}_{t,i}(r)) \nonumber\\
&\land(\forall t\in\mathsf C_\tau:\mathsf{CloseOK}_t) \nonumber\\
&\land(\forall(t,q,q')\in\mathsf A_\tau: \nonumber\\
&\hspace{24mm}\mathsf{AdvanceOK}_t(q,q')).
\label{eq:proof-aligned}
\end{align}
Here $\mathsf{IssueOK}$ composes the L1 eligibility relation in
Equation~\ref{eq:eligible} with L2a issuance of a bound, fresh, one-use
permission.  $\mathsf{ScreenOK}$ requires
Equation~\ref{eq:guard-qualified} exactly when
Equation~\ref{eq:guard-trigger} holds.  The property gives a checker-relative
execution-integrity objective under the stated TCB.  It does not prove the
checker complete, the dynamics model faithful to a real robot, or the resulting
trajectory free of hazards outside the registered predicates.
