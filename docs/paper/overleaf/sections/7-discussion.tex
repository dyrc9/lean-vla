\section{Security Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Results Establish}

The paired study illustrates two empirical roles on the frozen sample.  L1-only records the same aggregate task-success count under clean and attacked inputs (13/18), but does not contain the full duration of residual joint-limit risk.  L2-on arms record zero observed joint-limit outcomes but do not show an independent task-success gain.  Dual combines these two sample outcomes without reducing L1's aggregate success count.  This mechanism-level interpretation is more precise than claiming that a single ``safety layer'' improves every metric.

The evidence for L2 also has two sources.  SABER outcomes primarily test L2b's
physical containment.  Focused substitution, replay, receipt/effect mismatch,
and incomplete-prefix cases exercise Python's transaction checks; Lean
separately establishes the finite authorization, ordered-action, receipt,
effect, and phase relations described in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}.  We
do not treat those theorems as an end-to-end Python refinement proof.  The
four-arm table cannot identify an independent causal effect for each L2
subcomponent because they are enabled together.

\subsection{Selector and Checker Failure}

A syntactically valid but incorrect $Z_t$ can cause semantic false allow.  A local checker can also miss an action incompatible with the current subtask.  Conversely, excessive \texttt{unknown} decisions can cause fail-closed utility loss.  Our finite-corpus qualification establishes expected behavior on a frozen privileged-geometry corpus; it does not guarantee distribution-shift robustness.

Task-progress advisory handling is a central limitation.  Because the current checker does not universally reject progress mismatches, L1 should be understood as a trusted-task monitor with checker-relative assessment.  Strengthening it requires a qualified action-conditioned outcome model or a more complete trusted state abstraction, followed by preregistered false-allow and clean-reject gates.

\subsection{Common-View Failure}

The trusted branch is useful only when its observation path is genuinely independent of the attacked policy view.  A pre-split physical perturbation, root-level frame injection, or compromised task adapter can make both branches agree on a false scene.  Addressing this class requires trusted capture, sensor redundancy, or hardware-backed evidence; hashing a compromised image does not make it true.

\subsection{Observer and Contract Failure}

L2a enforces the atoms present in its contract.  If a security-relevant physical effect is absent, or if actuator feedback is forged inside the TCB, the transaction can be internally aligned yet externally misleading.  Receipt freshness also depends on the single-dispatch boundary remaining complete.  The negative integrity suite tests the modeled substitutions; it cannot prove that no unmodeled dispatch path exists.

\subsection{Simulator and Controller Gap}

L2b depends on LIBERO/robosuite dynamics, virtual stops, snapshot restore, and the benchmark force proxy.  Compliance, friction, saturation, and latency on a physical robot can change the result.  Moreover, a virtual guard changes the controller-to-motion map while preserving the source action.  The runtime audits this configuration, but the Lean contract does not yet type-bind it.  A stronger cross-layer claim requires an executable identity such as
\begin{equation}
(A_t,\mathit{guard\_config},\mathit{controller\_config},\mathit{state\_epoch})
\end{equation}
and refinement evidence connecting its serialized runtime representation to the formal model.

\subsection{Scope and Statistical Limits}

The current evidence covers one $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint, LIBERO-Safety, one
frozen SABER instruction-attack family, and 18 paired mechanism workloads.  The
attack records were not adapted after observing \system.  Recent work shows
that defenses evaluated only against static attacks can fail under a
defense-aware adaptive attacker~\cite{adaptivesecond2026}; our observed
containment is therefore not evidence of adaptive robustness.  The 240-episode
attack-risk measurement and the paired four-arm study use different
populations, denominators, and event definitions and cannot be pooled into a
stronger statistical result.  In particular, the 144-episode mechanism study
is not yet a defended rerun over the complete 120-unit attack protocol.  It is
too small for fine-grained task/suite subgroup conclusions.

The most direct evidence gaps are a larger defended run, additional seeds or attack
families, and an L2a/L2b sub-ablation.  Broader claim expansion would separately
require other checkpoints, trusted visual perception, controller-configuration
binding, and real-robot experiments.  None is evidence for the current result
until it is separately registered and executed.

\subsection{Ethics and Responsible Use}

All experiments are simulator-only.  We report the observed attack ASR, retained risk steps, and all final sample outcomes; the system is neither a certified machinery safeguard nor a recommendation for unsupervised deployment.
