\begin{abstract}
Vision--language--action (VLA) policies increasingly expose a minimal deployment
interface: images, robot state, and a natural-language task enter the model; a
chunk of continuous actions comes out.  This interface creates two security
gaps.  First, a compromised prompt or observation can change the numerical
trajectory without exposing a trustworthy plan that authorizes the change.
Second, checking a proposed action does not establish that the same action is
later dispatched, receipted, and realized.  Existing defenses typically stop
before the continuous action boundary or begin from an already trusted
command or trajectory.

We present \system, a consumer-side reference monitor for this action-only
boundary.  Its protected object is the exact source \actionblock proposed by
the VLA.  A trusted task/observation branch freezes the currently legal
semantic subtask before policy inference.  \lone binds a checker-relative
assessment to that subtask, observation, and exact block.  \ltwoa carries the
same identity through a fresh one-use authorization, ordered dispatch
receipts, observed effects, and phase advance; Lean checks the abstract finite
binding and phase semantics.  When joint state approaches a limit, \ltwob
screens at most two temporary virtual-guard configurations against registered
margin, restoration, identity, and force gates without changing or resampling
the source action.

We first successfully reproduce SABER's constraint-violation instruction
attack: 39 of 86 clean-eligible units exhibit a new risk transition
(45.35\%, 95\% base-pair cluster-bootstrap CI: [32.93\%, 57.78\%]).  We then
run a paired four-arm study over 18 held-out task/initialization pairs and 144
episodes.  Under attack, VLA-only and L2-only succeed on 11/18 tasks, whereas
L1-only and Dual succeed on 13/18; their joint-limit violation-episode counts
are 4/18, 1/18, 0/18, and 0/18.  Screening reaches 39.79\,ms maximum and
18.30\,ms p95, with no 100\,ms miss.  These results support
checker-relative, cross-layer execution integrity for the frozen attack family
and simulator setting; they do not establish complete semantic correctness,
hardware attestation, hard-real-time behavior, or general robot safety.
\end{abstract}
