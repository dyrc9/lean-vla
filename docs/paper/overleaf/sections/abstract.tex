\begin{abstract}
Vision--language--action (\vla) systems turn language and observations into
continuous robot actions.  Surveyed defenses protect different segments of this
path, yet their stated mechanisms do not jointly bind independently anchored
task authority to the exact newly generated \actionblock, its ordered dispatch,
and registered execution evidence.  This leaves an intent--action authorization
gap and an action--execution realization gap.

\system establishes a scoped alignment chain across the two gaps at the
action-only consumer boundary.  In the first stage, \lone performs a
checker-relative assessment of the exact online \actionblock against trusted
task intent.  In the second stage, \ltwo carries an admitted block to execution:
\ltwoa binds a
one-use ordered transaction and its evidence, while \ltwob qualifies the same
source action under a registered joint-boundary trigger.  The \proofaligned
property composes issuance, dispatch, evidence-closure, phase-advance, and
triggered-guard obligations; Lean checks selected abstract transaction and
phase relations.

An independent study reproduces the SABER constraint-violation instruction
attack on OpenPI $\pi_{0.5}$ in LIBERO-Safety.  Among 86 clean-eligible units,
39 develop a new physical-risk transition under attack, giving an observed ASR
of 45.35\%.  A separate paired four-arm study provides no evidence of a broad
risk reduction, but none of its 237 valid attacked \ltwoenabled traces contains
a joint-limit step.  The claims remain checker-relative and limited to the
registered joint boundary, frozen attack family, checkpoint, and simulator.
\end{abstract}
