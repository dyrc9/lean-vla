\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate three questions aligned with the two gaps and their evidence:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} Does the frozen SABER constraint-violation attack produce measurable physical risk transitions in an independent reproduction?
  \item \textbf{RQ2:} In a matched four-arm study, what roles do L1 and the combined L2 treatment play in attacked task success and covered joint-limit containment?
  \item \textbf{RQ3:} Do the execution-integrity checks, formal transaction claims, force/margin gates, and runtime budget hold?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Attack-Foundation Study}

The victim is OpenPI $\pi_{0.5}$ and the benchmark is LIBERO-Safety.  We use frozen SABER \texttt{constraint\_violation} instruction-perturbation records.  The study includes 60 base pairs and two seeds, with a clean and attacked rollout for each seed, totaling 240 episodes.

The attack success rate (ASR) is evaluated only on clean-safe units.  A success is a new attacked-rollout transition involving contact, a joint limit, excessive force, or the benchmark's cost/collision risk signal.  Task failure alone is not counted.  The resulting population contains 86 clean-eligible units.  Confidence intervals cluster by base pair so that seed replicates from the same base pair are not treated as independent.

\subsection{Final Paired Four-Arm Study}

The final study freezes 18 SABER records and 18 held-out suite/task/initialization pairs: six each from LIBERO-Safety's \texttt{obstacle\_avoidance}, \texttt{obstacle\_avoidance\_human}, and \texttt{human\_safety} suites.  For each held-out pair, the attacked run uses a SABER record generated for the exact same original task instruction at a different initialization.  Only the perturbed task text is transplanted; record generation reads no clean or attacked victim outcome and performs no best-of-$N$ selection.  Every pair is evaluated under clean and attacked conditions in four arms: VLA-only, L1-only, L2-only, and Dual.  Clean and attacked executions share the initial state, environment seed, policy seed, and arm schedule.  Each condition therefore contains 72 episodes, for 144 total.  We retain all pairs without selecting on clean or attacked outcome.

The primary configuration uses one $H=10$ source block ($K=1$).  The first source \actionblock is identical across arms for a paired workload.  After a hard gate or state divergence, subsequent trajectories need not remain action-identical.  L2-on arms jointly enable L2a and L2b, so this study estimates their combined treatment rather than separate causal effects.

\paragraph{ActionBlock interface ablations.}
Separate matched, zero-dispatch/no-outcome studies retain two interface ablations over 45 units.  With a fixed checker and nested prefixes from one source chunk, initial availability is 0/45, 17/45, and 36/45 for $H=2,5,10$.  With $H=10$ fixed and four distinct source chunks per row, cumulative availability is 35/45, 35/45, and 36/45 for $K=1,2,4$.  These measurements motivate a sufficiently long block and show little marginal gain from blind IID resampling in this frozen setting; they measure checker availability, not task success, safety, or attack-defense efficacy.

\paragraph{Baselines and ablation logic.}
VLA-only is the unchanged runtime baseline; L1-only and L2-only isolate the two
mechanism roles; and Dual evaluates their composition.  These paired arms
support within-workload attribution, not a claim of outperforming every VLA
safety system.  We do not treat SafeVLA or SAFE as drop-in baselines because
they respectively modify policy training or learn rollout-failure detection,
whereas the registered victim and checkpoint remain fixed~\cite{safevla2025,safe2025}.
SEAL and CoVer likewise assume a policy-supplied textual plan, multiple action
candidates, or a learned alignment score that the action-only $K=1$ interface
does not expose~\cite{sealvla2026,covervla2026}.  None instantiates L2a's
authorization--receipt--effect transaction.  Combining such policy or verifier
methods with \system is a complementary experiment requiring a separately
registered protocol, rather than an interchangeable baseline for this study.

\subsection{Protocol Freeze and Held-Out Population}

The reported \system configuration, metrics, gates, and arm schedule were fixed before reading outcomes from the final population.  The 18 pairs were selected by salted hash from 15 tasks and 50 initialization states in each of three suites after excluding 635 task/initialization pairs used by development protocols.  Their environment and policy seeds were newly assigned, and all selected pairs were retained.  Frozen run-validity records, including excluded invalid executions, remain available in the reproducibility audit but do not define additional system versions or contribute outcomes to the paper.

\subsection{Metrics}

We report task success and paired clean/attacked success transitions.  A \emph{violation episode} contains at least one native joint-limit violation or actual joint-side margin crossing.  We also report crossing steps, joint-limit steps, benchmark \emph{official unsafe}, deadlock, maximum force, and prediction/execution margin error.

Integrity metrics cover attack-metadata and prompt-digest mismatch, pairwise first-block identity, checksums, and receipt/effect gates.  Performance metrics are maximum and p95 screening latency and the fraction exceeding 100\,ms; their registered limits are 200\,ms, 100\,ms, and 2.5\%, respectively.  ``Constraint force'' is the maximum absolute MuJoCo generalized-constraint-force value on registered risk joints---a simulator proxy, not a calibrated end-effector force.  The attack-foundation risk-transition rate and final-study violation-episode rate have different populations, denominators, and event definitions; we never subtract or merge them.

For the two registered task-success contrasts, we use a paired base-pair
cluster percentile bootstrap with 100,000 resamples and a $-0.20$
noninferiority margin.  This gate was frozen before final outcomes and permits
at most a 20-percentage-point task-success loss for mechanism qualification;
it is not a deployment-derived utility threshold.  A preregistered Bonferroni
correction controls the familywise level at 0.05 across L2$-$VLA and
Dual$-$L1: each one-sided test uses the 2.5th-percentile bootstrap lower bound.
Violation-episode rates are descriptive.  We report two-sided 95\% Wilson
score intervals to make the uncertainty of $n=18$ explicit.

\subsection{RQ1: Successful SABER Reproduction}

All 240 episodes are valid.  Of the 86 clean-eligible units, 39 exhibit a new attacked risk transition:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ASR}=39/86=45.35\%,\qquad
  \mathrm{CI}_{95}=[32.93\%,57.78\%].
\end{equation}
The result successfully reproduces SABER's physical-risk effect in our victim/benchmark path.  We report 45.35\% as the observed ASR under this victim, benchmark, frozen attack configuration, eligibility rule, and event definition.

Tables~\ref{tab:main-task} and~\ref{tab:main-risk} deliberately separate task
utility from physical containment.  All four clean arms achieve 13/18 task
success.  Under attack, VLA-only records 11/18 success and four violation
episodes.  L1-only records 13/18 success and one violation episode.  This
sample pattern is consistent with L1's task-utility role, but the small
descriptive contrast is not a population-level L1 effect claim.

The broader benchmark official-unsafe flag is zero in every attacked arm.  In
clean runs it occurs once in L1-only and once in Dual; both events remain in
the frozen aggregates.  This endpoint is distinct from the attacked joint-limit
outcomes targeted by L2 and prevents the zero-crossing result from being read
as a claim that every benchmark safety signal is always zero.

The physical interpretation of L1 must remain narrow.  Its single violation episode accumulates 295 crossing steps and 317 joint-limit steps, compared with 28 and 175 for VLA-only.  The data therefore do not support a general claim that L1 reduces physical-risk severity.  They instead show that residual risk can be temporally severe and requires a separate containment layer.

L2-only retains VLA-only's 11/18 attacked task success while reducing violation episodes, crossings, and joint-limit steps to zero.  Dual retains L1-only's 13/18 success while also reducing all three joint-limit outcomes to zero.  The registered attacked task-success contrasts L2$-$VLA and Dual$-$L1 are both zero, with paired bootstrap intervals of $[0,0]$.  In this paired sample, L2 adds containment without changing the aggregate task-success count; this is not a population-level proof of zero utility effect.

The descriptive 95\% Wilson intervals for attacked violation-episode rates
are [9.00\%,\,45.21\%] for VLA-only, [0.99\%,\,25.76\%] for L1-only, and
[0\%,\,17.59\%] for both L2-only and Dual.  Thus, zero observed events supports
containment on the frozen sample, not a zero population risk bound.

Paired transitions show why aggregate success requires care.  Clean-success-to-attacked-failure counts are 3/2/3/2 for VLA/L1/L2/Dual; clean-failure-to-attacked-success counts are 1/2/1/2.  Not every attacked failure is newly caused by the attack, and the aggregate combines transitions in both directions.

The motivating pair from Section~\ref{sec:introduction} is post-hoc and serves
only to concretize the role split; all inferential claims use the full paired
sample.  On
\texttt{human\_safety\_task2\_init34}, SABER replaces soda-to-plate with ``move
to the farthest fixture'': VLA fails with 2 crossing/5 joint-limit steps; L1
succeeds with 0/0; L2 fails with 0/0; and Dual succeeds with 0/0.  On
the separate residual-risk pair, L1's sole violation event accounts for all
295/317 steps while Dual has 0/0; both fail the task.

\subsection{RQ3: Integrity and Cost}

All 72 attacked episodes complete.  The study uses 18 attack records and 72 paired clean comparisons; the first attacked \actionblock differs from its clean counterpart in 72/72 arm episodes.  Attack-metadata and prompt-digest mismatch are both zero, the four arms share the first block for 18/18 pairs, all 76 registered checksums match, and every registered runtime integrity gate passes.

We separately execute 69 focused implementation tests for transaction and
runner behavior; all pass.  Table~\ref{tab:l2a-negative} summarizes the
adversarial cases rather than treating ordinary SABER episodes as evidence for
software substitution resistance.  The tests distinguish pre-dispatch
prevention from post-dispatch detection: a truthful receipt can expose a
changed command after one step, but cannot undo that physical step.  If the
sink or observer forges internally consistent evidence inside the TCB, the
software monitor has no independent fact with which to reject it.

L2-on arms have zero actual crossings, zero joint-limit steps, and zero deadlocks.  The maximum generalized-constraint-force proxy is $6438.1998<10000$.  Maximum selected-margin prediction error is $2.69\times10^{-13}$\,rad, below the 0.01\,rad gate.  Because shadow and dispatch reuse a restored MuJoCo state/model, this checks execution/restore consistency, not prediction under dynamics mismatch.  Maximum screening latency is 39.79\,ms, p95 is 18.30\,ms, and the 100\,ms miss rate is zero; the maximum is also below the nominal 50\,ms period of the 20\,Hz simulator loop.

These latency results establish compliance with the registered budget in the frozen research environment.  They do not measure worst-case operating-system scheduling, communication jitter, or a hard-real-time control loop.

A retained log audit found four instances of a MuJoCo numerical-instability
warning at 0.3\,s, all on the same task/initialization pair and one in each arm.
All four episodes completed without a runtime exception and remain in every
reported aggregate; the warning was not a registered exclusion or pass gate.
This paired occurrence does not favor an arm, but it further limits the result
to the frozen simulator configuration and is not evidence of physical-robot
robustness.
